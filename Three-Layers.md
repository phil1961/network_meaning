# Three Layers

Voice session, 2026-09-30. Phil's framing; Claude's phrasing where noted.

## The layers

The current map — nodes and relationships derived from what a person said —
is only one layer. Call it the **interaction layer**. Two more sit with it.

**Foundation layer.** Not derived from anyone's words. Authored in advance: a
designed set of objects and relationships. Moral precepts. This is where the
theology skeleton lives — the two axes, the three virtues, sacrifice at the
crossing. Fixed before anyone talks to the tool. The user can click down into
it.

**Environment layer.** Particular to the person: their job, their family,
where they live, the political, social and economic situation they feel
themselves to be in. This is the one layer that can neither be designed in
advance nor derived from the ideas alone — it has to be asked for.

**Interaction layer.** What the person actually said. Verbatim spans, ideas
cited back to them, relationships between those ideas. What the app already
does.

## How they stack

The individual exists **between two frameworks**. The foundation is the floor.
The environment rests on top of it and is what the person actually touches day
to day.

So the environment is never neutral ground — it is already a rendering of the
foundation. A job, a family, a political moment: each one instantiates or
violates the precepts underneath in some particular way. (Claude's reading of
Phil's framing.)

The diagnostic that follows: not "what does this person believe," but **where
is their situation bending the foundation, and do they know it.**

## Where motivation comes from

Phil: it is the person's existence *between* the two layers that motivation
draws from. Pain, pleasure. He is moving through the system along a vector,
against resistance — or with little resistance, or with strong resistance.

Claude's restatement, which Phil can accept or cut: motivation is not a
property of the person, it is the **gap** — the friction between what the
foundation says and what the environment affords. No gap, no motion. Large
gap, and the felt result is pain, which is the sensation of resistance against
the vector being travelled.

Consequence for the tool: it never has to ask "what motivates you." It reads
motivation off the **distance between the layers**. Pain becomes a signal with
a direction rather than a symptom to be soothed.

This is plausibly the stuck-versus-plastic terrain from the earlier vector
work, but grounded in structure rather than asserted.

## Open
