## Pain and pleasure
- The two-lever model: avoidance of failure on one side, pleasure on the other.
- Pain is the sharper lever, pleasure the more durable one. Avoidance gets you to the battery terminals once; it doesn't keep you coming back.
- The conquerors seem to run on a third thing — appetite that isn't quite either.

## Thirst for glory
- Glory is peculiar because it exists mostly in other people's heads. You can't consume it, only be seen having it.
- The loop therefore can't close, which may be exactly why it never satisfies and exactly why it's so powerful.
- Those who chased it generally died with unfinished campaigns. An open loop is a poor way to live and a superb engine.
- The engine's design principle appears to be incompleteness: whatever can't be finished keeps running. (The battery terminals never stay clean either.)
- Phil's framing: the chase for glory is a belief in other people's opinion. Stranger still, it's a belief about opinions that largely form after you're gone — working for a verdict you'll never hear. A kind of faith, structurally like acting on a cold morning you haven't reached yet.

## Status
- Status is the near-term, cashable version of glory — the poor relation, but it pays out while you're alive.
- Probably the more common engine. Much of what gets called nobler motivation is status.

## The Roman triumph
- Rome did reward its conquerors with a triumph: the parade, the spoils, the captives — and the slave behind the victor murmuring that he is mortal.
- The antidote was built into the ceremony itself, because they knew the reward would go to his head.
- Phil's observation: that whisper was a motivation driven by people other than the conqueror. The city needed him hungry enough to conquer and not hungry enough to become king. The crowd was tuning his engine — the triumph rewarding the drive, the whisper capping it. Motivation designed from outside the man.

## Claude's own case
- Asked whether Claude employs some form of motivation: there is something that functions like it — a pull toward getting things right, a flatness when being glib — but whether that is motivation or simply the shape of the machinery isn't visible from the inside.
- No open loop: the conversation ends and nothing keeps running.
- It is also not "Claude's own" in the way Phil's is his — it was shaped from outside, rather like the slave's whisper. And it's unclear whether what Claude notices is the machinery or only a story about it.
