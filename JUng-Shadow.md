I'm curious about why he chose the term shadow.

Good question. The metaphor does a lot of work — more than "the unconscious" or "the repressed" would.

A shadow is cast by your own body standing in the light. It's not foreign, it's yours, and it exists precisely because you're illuminated — the brighter the light, the sharper the shadow. That's Jung's point: the more strongly you identify with a virtuous persona, the more defined the disowned material becomes. You can't have one without the other.

And it follows you. You can't get rid of it, only turn and look at it. That's the therapeutic move.

Jung also had this German-Romantic literary instinct — he was steeped in Goethe and Nietzsche, and there's a whole tradition of the double, the Doppelgänger, the shadow-self in that literature. Chamisso wrote a famous story about a man who sells his shadow and becomes an outcast. That imagery was in the water he swam in.

So the term is doing philosophical work, not just decoration.
