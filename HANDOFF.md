## 1. What this project is

A semi-systematic theology built on two New Testament anchors, worked out
through a tool that helps record and round out thoughts. The app and UI are
window dressing. The theology is the deliverable. The hope is that it becomes
something of value to others.

**Primary intended benefit** (Phil's words): "to anchor a highly meaningful and
motivational attitude in the pursuit of these two tenets," for himself and for
others.

**Theological center** (Phil's words): "I am absolutely convinced that Christ
performed his sacrifice with these two tenets in mind. All of man's existence
is looped into that faith being true. It's my faith in him and his sacrificial
act that makes my life and the lives of others useful, meaningful, and
existentially hopeful."

## 2. The two anchors

### Anchor one: the Great Commandment

Matthew 22:37-40 (KJV):

> Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul,
> and with all thy mind. This is the first and great commandment. And the
> second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. On these
> two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.

Parallels: Mark 12:29-31, Luke 10:27. Sources: Deuteronomy 6:5 (the Shema) and
Leviticus 19:18. Paul restates the claim in Romans 13:8-10 ("love is the
fulfilling of the law") and Galatians 5:14.

Textual notes to hold accurately:

| Source | Faculties listed |
|---|---|
| Deuteronomy 6:5 | heart, soul, might |
| Matthew 22:37 | heart, soul, mind |
| Mark 12:30 | heart, soul, mind, strength |
| Luke 10:27 | heart, soul, strength, mind |
| Phil's phrasing | heart, mind, body |

"Body" is a gloss, closest to Mark's "strength." Worth deciding whether to keep
it as an interpretive choice or quote one text verbatim.

**What the anchor supplies: geometry.** "Hang" (Greek *krematai*) is a
structural claim. Two root nodes; every other law and prophecy is an arc
derived from them. Love of God is the vertical axis. Love of neighbor is the
horizontal axis.

### Anchor two: Paul's triad

1 Corinthians 13:13 (KJV):

> And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these
> is charity.

Context matters. Chapter 13 sits inside Paul's treatment of spiritual gifts
(chapters 12 through 14). Verse 8: "Charity never faileth: but whether there be
prophecies, they shall fail." Verse 10: "when that which is perfect is come."
Verse 12: "now we see through a glass, darkly." Faith and hope belong to the
time of not-yet-seeing. Charity alone survives the end of time.

Paul uses the triad elsewhere: 1 Thessalonians 1:3 ("work of faith, labour of
love, patience of hope"), 1 Thessalonians 5:8, Colossians 1:4-5.

Textual note: Paul says "greatest," not "first," and lists charity last. Both
facts are useful. The sentence order is the sequence a being in time passes
through. The rank is by what endures.

**What the anchor supplies: ordering in time.** Faith and hope are the virtues
of a creature moving through time. Charity is the terminus and the ground.

### The word that links both anchors

Both use the same Greek root: the verb *agapao* in Matthew 22:37 and 39, the
noun *agape* throughout 1 Corinthians 13. The Vulgate rendered Paul's noun as
*caritas*, which is where the King James "charity" comes from. Keeping
"charity" rather than "love" preserves the distinction from *eros* and
*philia*. Both anchors are one word. That is a stuck node.

## 3. The center: the sacrifice

The corpus repeatedly points toward "the good" as an untakeable objective. Phil
has now located that good in a historical act. In the system's own terms, the
sacrifice is the hardest terrain there is. Interpretations of it are plastic.
The event is stuck. The tool never argues for it or explains it away. It holds
it as the fixed root every other node hangs from.

**Where the two commandments become one act.** Obedience unto death is love of
God with everything. Laying down life for friends (John 15:13: "Greater love
hath no man than this") is love of neighbor at its limit. The cross is the one
place the vertical and horizontal axes cross.

**Charity is first in causal order, not only in rank.** 1 John 4:19: "We love
him, because he first loved us." Romans 5:8: "while we were yet sinners, Christ
died for us." Faith and hope are our response to a love that preceded them.
This gives Phil's phrase "charity is first" a second, stronger meaning than
Paul's "greatest."

**Hope enters here by name.** 1 Corinthians 15:14-19, two chapters after the
triad: "if Christ be not risen ... your faith is also vain ... If in this life
only we have hope in Christ, we are of all men most miserable." Phil's
"existentially hopeful" is that passage. Hope is not optimism. It is a
furnished future the sacrifice makes real enough to live in now.

**The traceback is instituted.** Phil's test for any anchor is whether it
invites the person to trace back to the reasons or forbids it. "This do in
remembrance of me" is the traceback built into the center itself.

## 4. The meaningful vector

Named once, explicitly, in Fascinating-Discovery.md:

> "meaning" is always a vector: it's the map straining toward the node it
> hasn't drawn yet.

Every document in the corpus is either a component of this vector or a test
case for it. The vector has four parts.

**Origin: the seam.** Meaning starts where an expectation breaks.
Pure-Consciousness builds this as Bobby-and-the-milk: "Bobby went to the store
to get milk" is flat until one fact you bring to it (Bobby hates milk; Bobby
has no car) detonates a node or an arc. Defining-Consciousness finds the same
thing in persons: the signal lives in the mismatch between behavior, imputed
inner life, and self-report. "Surprise is the fuel."

**Direction: toward the good, now named as charity.** The vector does not point
at resolution in general. Pure-Consciousness ends on the bank robber versus
Paul: both silence the alarm, but only the one aimed at an untakeable good is
faith rather than recklessness. Isaiah's Life makes the endpoint historical:
prophecy as a seed crystal, Jesus as the emergent term the whole system was
resolving toward.

**Terrain: stuck versus plastic.** Fascinating-Discovery calls it granite and
clay. Meaning-Generator makes it a data model: verbatim text and stated facts
are stuck, inferences are plastic, and a plastic chain that contradicts a stuck
node dies however elegant it is. Probing is how you learn which is which. "It
takes probing and experimentation and leaking out to find out just how real and
what the terrain is."

**Budget: scarce attention.** Energy is spent in proportion to surprise. A
branch continues while it yields new distinctions and stops when it only
decorates. This is the stopping rule in Meaning-Generator and the "miser's
attention" in Fascinating-Discovery.

## 5. The three virtues mapped onto the mechanics

| Virtue | Mechanical role | Corpus status |
|---|---|---|
| Faith | The un-violated node. The step onto water before proof. The watcher, re-furnished so the wrong things stop firing alarms. | Fully built in Pure-Consciousness.md |
| Hope | The forward direction of the vector. The furnished future the child already lives in ("grandma's after dinner"). The horizon placed rather than dreamed. | Present but unnamed. Isaiah's Life is about it. Needs its own treatment. |
| Charity | The destination and the ground. The good that is "out there and in there." The readout of growth. | Named everywhere as "the good," never yet as love. This session made the identification. |

### Faith, as already built

Key claims from Pure-Consciousness, in order:

1. Faith is a function, not a feeling. "The mind's capacity to treat a good,
   unrealized future as reliable enough to act on, before any proof arrives."
2. Faith is furnished. Memory supplies material, imagination projects it, faith
   commits to it.
3. Faith habituates into furniture. "The strongest faith is the kind you've
   forgotten is faith at all." The car starts. The floor holds.
4. Peter on the water (Matthew 14:22-33) is the diagnostic. Faith fails the
   instant it is converted back into analysis. "Thinking is corrosive to it."
5. The mustard seed (Matthew 17:20) is about kind, not quantity. Deliberate
   aim, non-deliberate execution. Note: the mustard seed moves a mountain; the
   mountain cast into the sea is Matthew 21:21 and Mark 11:23. Two sayings.
6. Faith is realization: the crossing from possible to actual. Phil corrected
   "creation" to "realization." Keep that word.
7. Come, stay, leave. Three motions of everything that passes through a life,
   all realization, all faith. The hardest faith is letting something leave.
8. The word "faith" exists for the edge, not the morning. "The word exists for
   the mountain, not the morning."
9. The field. Unbidden things arrive from a commons one is a member of, not
   from a basement in oneself. "Meness is a cut. Usness is what's there before
   the knife." Peter walked while joined to Jesus's consciousness and sank when
   he re-individuated.
10. The watcher is saturated, not pure. The alarm defends the furniture of the
    self. "Wrong means dangerous to who I am."
11. Conversion re-furnishes the watcher. Paul is the finished demonstration:
    the lion's mouth is deep water that finally holds.
12. Home is faith made of walls. Jesus has no walls (Matthew 8:20). Sleep is
    the nightly surrender.
13. Faith turned inward: trust that the self doing the trusting can be
    changed. "Realization aimed at the realizer."
14. Growth is measured by the exterior: more at ease, less gripped, more loving
    to friends, neighbors, family. "By their fruits ye shall know them."
15. Original sin as inherited misalignment: misaimed alarms laid down young,
    compounding into structure. Redemption as the long re-aiming. *Hamartia*
    means missing the mark.
16. Grace escapes Peter's trap. "You can't fully clear yourself by yourself,
    because the one doing the clearing is the very thing that needs clearing."

### "As thyself" is doing more than ethics

Defining-Consciousness describes how you model another mind: from your own
experience, then checked against their report, with the mismatches as the
signal. That is the same clause. Loving your neighbor as yourself is both the
command and the only epistemic method available for knowing a neighbor at all.
It also closes the loop reached in Pure-Consciousness: the good "out there is
also in there."

## 6. Design principles for the tool

All derived from constraints the corpus already set.

1. **Motivation comes from direction plus landing.** An attitude is
   motivational when the vector has a fixed direction and a place to land. The
   two commandments supply direction. The probe rule supplies landing: every
   session ends on a small, cheap act in the world whose result the person
   cannot control. For this theology the probe is nearly always an act toward
   a neighbor.
2. **The tool aims, it does not execute.** "Deliberate aim, non-deliberate
   execution." The tool works before and after the act, never during it. It
   does not narrate the step while the person is on the water.
3. **Anchoring without stuckness is the flattering mirror.** An engine with no
   resistance will build a meaningful story around a harmful plan as fluently
   as a good one. A motivational tool is the most exposed to this failure
   because motivation feels like success. Verbatim scripture is stuck. What
   the person has actually done is stuck. Interpretations are plastic and die
   when they contradict the text or the record.
4. **Motivation is measured by fruit, not fervor.** Ease, less fear, more love
   toward the people nearby. The tool must not reward intensity of feeling.
5. **The traceback stays open.** Every derivation shows its path from the
   person's particular back to one of the two commands or to the sacrifice, in
   plain language, so the person can check it and own it. "The versions that
   sealed the traceback curdled."
6. **Keep the raw particulars.** Each generalization discards the specific
   case. Keep the person's own words beside the abstraction and check at the
   end: does the output still fit this person? That is where a generalized
   answer fails and a person feels unseen.
7. **Graph vocabulary stays behind the curtain** in anything published. The
   architecture organizes; the prose stays human.

## 7. The Meaning Generator, as specified so far

From Meaning-Generator.md. This is the only file in the corpus that is a spec
rather than a conversation.

**Energy as budget.** Each inquiry gets a fixed budget (LLM calls, tokens, wall
time). Each violation gets a surprise score. Budget is allocated in proportion
to surprise. Stop a branch when a new expansion produces no new entity,
relation, or question.

**Probing before committing.** Cheap shallow passes first, one hop per
violation. Measure which branches come back rich. Commit deep chains only to
rich branches.

**Being in time.** The frame persists across turns. Each turn records bets
(expectations for the next turn). A missed bet is a new violation with fresh
budget. Two surfacing channels: directed (retrieval on the current question)
and unbidden (low-budget background pass that surfaces only above threshold).

**Stuck versus plastic.** Every node marked. Stuck: verbatim source texts, what
the person has stated, fixed facts of their situation, anything in the physical
world. Plastic: interpretations, generalizations, analogies, inferences. Plastic
can be rewired freely. Stuck can only be interpreted. Plastic chains that
contradict stuck nodes die.

**Direction lands on terrain.** A response about direction ends on a probe.
The world's answer comes back as a stuck node next session, and it is the only
input the system gets that it did not generate itself.

**Minimal state:**

```
frame_graph       nodes, edges, defaults
violations[]      surprise score, branch budget, yield history
bets[]            expectations for the next turn
budget_remaining
raw_particulars   unabstracted user text
node.kind         stuck | plastic
node.source       text_id | user_stated | world_probe | inferred
probes[]          action proposed, result reported
```

**Open design question, unanswered:** does the budget belong to the system
(fixed compute per turn) or to the person (pull-driven, sustained across turns
for someone in an unresolved state)? Phil's lattice model suggests the second.
Nothing in the corpus closes it.

## 8. Build strategy

Phil sees at least three prongs. One has been described.

**Prong one: the recording tool.** Server-based, internet-accessible, posted on
one of Phil's servers. Records his thoughts, probably by voice given how the
corpus was made. Provides feedback that encourages "a rounder explanation."

How the feedback should work: the frame graph notices which default slots a
statement left empty, which arc was asserted without saying how, and asks the
one question that would fill it. Not critique, not agreement. The question a
good listener asks. Every entry can trace back to one of the two commands or to
the sacrifice, so the anchor stays in view while writing.

Environment note: Phil's servers are Windows, IIS is in use elsewhere in his
projects. Not yet decided for this tool.

**Prong two: the magic.** Whatever can be incorporated and meaningfully
applied to the meaning engine itself. The Meaning Generator spec in Section 7
is the starting point. Comes after prong one.

**Prong three: the UI build-out.** Comes last. One of its products is a
diagram generator. Phil expects the diagramming ability to be useful for the
data-gathering prong too, so it should be designed as a shared component from
the start rather than a UI-only feature.

Diagramming fits the system naturally: the frame graph is already nodes and
arcs, stuck and plastic nodes can be drawn differently, and a diagram of an
entry showing which slots are empty is itself the "rounder explanation"
feedback in visual form. Fuller's tetrahedron (Tetrahedron-Visualization.md)
may be the seed for a geometry of the two axes and three virtues.

## 10. Lineage

Phil is on a road with good company, and that is a feature.

- Augustine, *Enchiridion ad Laurentium de fide, spe et caritate* (c. 421):
  a handbook organizing all Christian doctrine under faith, hope, and love.
- Augustine, *De Doctrina Christiana*, Book I: all scripture teaches love of
  God and love of neighbor.
- Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* II-II: faith (qq. 1-16), hope (qq. 17-22),
  charity (qq. 23-46). Charity is greatest because it attains God as he is;
  faith and hope attain him as source of truth and of good to come.

The doctrine is not where the value to others lies. The contribution is the
mechanics: how these three virtues operate inside a mind moving through time,
with expectation, surprise, terrain hardness, and misaimed alarms. Nobody in
that lineage had Bobby and the milk.

## 19. Dragging a box, a Map view that fits the window, and sign-in counts (2026-10-01, app 0.11.0)

Three requests from Phil the same night, after trying 0.10.0: "Yes, I also

- **Dragging a single box.** Drag any box but the middle one and it stays
  where it is dropped. It is an action step, `place` (`{id, around, dx,
  dy}`), kept in `st.places` per middle item as a move from the layout's
  own position, so it is saved, rewinds and branches. **Tidy** in the
  controls puts that middle's boxes back (`{around, reset: true}`).
  Dragging the middle box or the empty part of the diagram moves the whole
  diagram, which makes no step. A `place` step does not make a Help
  analysis count as stale. On a read-only sample the first drag starts the
  person's own copy, like any other action. Mouse or pen only: a finger
  scrolls the page.
- **The Map view fits the window.** Measured before the change: the
  diagram was as tall as its width made it, so on a 1366 by 768 screen the
  page ran 240 pixels past the window, and more on a larger screen. Now
  `fitMap()` in `src/client/60-map.js` gives the diagram the height that
  is left in the window, and the panel beside it scrolls inside itself. At
  100% the drawing is fitted to that frame but never below 0.72 screen
  pixels to a layout unit (about 9-pixel words). On a short window that
  leaves part of a world map outside the frame; the frame's edge then says
  "more below" (or above, left, right). The key to the diagram is now a
  fold: open on a tall window, closed on a short one, and remembered once
  the person changes it. The body no longer overflows the window by its
  own margin.
- **Sign-in counts.** `sql/004-logins.sql` adds `login_count`,
  `first_login_at` and `last_login_at` to `users`. `auth.recordLogin()`
  is called after a good sign-in and after sign-up. If the count cannot be
  written the person still gets in, and the log says so. The Admin tab
  shows "signed in N times · first … · last …". Counting starts at this
  migration: earlier sign-ins were not recorded. A session that is still
  good is not a sign-in, so with 30-day cookies the count is of sign-ins,
  not of visits.

  live site, and the page is read from disk on every request, while the
  server keeps the code it started with. Phil tried dragging a box while
  the new page was already live and the server did not yet know the
